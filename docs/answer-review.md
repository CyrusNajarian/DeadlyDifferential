# Clue-by-clue answer review

Reviewed all **95 symptoms/signs and 114 diagnoses** against all **19 playable clue/person screens**: **3,971 approval decisions**. The checked-in Excel workbook contains the resulting Yes/No matrix and drives the default build.

The subsequent reference consolidation leaves **94 symptom/sign headings and 107 diagnosis headings**. Related subtype names remain searchable and are listed within those headings. Their prior approvals migrate to the main entry; the original review scope above includes those related diagnoses.

## Review criteria

- A clue-level Yes means a reasonable description, mechanism or differential for that evidence. It does not mean that every typical feature is present or that the diagnosis is the best explanation for all clues.
- For the inferred culprit clues, motor/sensory/tone/attention mechanisms may explain an action. For direct suspect photos, accept findings that can actually be seen; do not invent reflexes, fasciculations, eye movements or measured muscle tone.
- Prescription/history-only clues retain no scored symptoms. A medication indication, a consequence of taking it and an unrelated comorbidity are different explanations.
- A No is a game approval decision for the evidence supplied, not a claim that a patient with that disease could never develop the symptom. Common direct explanations were preferred to chains of rare complications.
- The explicit author-requested parkinsonian-family acceptance rule overrides subtype differences. It applies even when a different family member is the closer clinical fit; it is not a clinical-equivalence statement.

## Catalog and scoring changes

Glioma, glioblastoma, meningioma and brain metastasis now resolve to the single Brain tumor entry. Intracranial mass remains separate, since non-tumor lesions can also form a mass. Retired searches and stored IDs are retained/migrated.

The seven parkinsonian entries are accepted together whenever any member is accepted, including the final Dr. Charcoal assignment. Other movement diagnoses, such as essential tremor, do not inherit that final assignment credit.

The review adds **424 clue-level approvals** and removes infectious encephalitis from the navigation-scan/sample email: uncomplicated encephalitis is not a usual focal navigation-guided tissue-procedure explanation. Infection remains represented by a focal brain abscess. These counts include the requested parkinsonian-family extensions.

Asymmetric short/dragging steps support weakness, clearance, tone, slowness, coordination, proprioceptive and painful-gait alternatives. Added lower-limb nerve/root, muscle and hydrocephalus possibilities. Isolated arm neuropathies do not account for the walking clue.

### C03 · The Key & Safe · Scene Inspection

## Important anatomical/clinical boundaries

- Spinal-cord-only transverse myelitis is not used to explain speech abnormalities or a cortical spatial-attention/visual-field pattern.
- Radial neuropathy is not accepted for the photographed intrinsic-hand wasting: the intrinsic hand muscles are supplied by the median/ulnar nerves. It can remain a key-dropping/hand-function differential in a different clue.
- The skin photographs do not establish sun sensitivity; the sun-flare email does. A symptom from one clue is not silently transferred into another.
- Prednisone does not establish weakness, cerebral edema or raised intracranial pressure. GBS and inclusion body myositis are not treated as routine prednisone-responsive indications. Steroid myopathy is an adverse effect, not a primary prescribing indication.
- Fluctuating ptosis is not treated as proof of diplopia, anisocoria or ophthalmoplegia. ALS is not added to the eyelid clue simply because it can cause limb/bulbar weakness.

## Final culprit feedback

Only the suspect is selected. The selector has no diagnosis description or lock-warning paragraph. After locking an incorrect choice, the app retains its overall explanation and adds five assessments—choking, footsteps, safe/key, voicemail and medication—for that suspect’s confirmed diagnosis. All six final diagnoses have a complete five-clue comparison. The final debrief continues to explain only culprit clues.

The MG voicemail comparison explicitly uses the early-morning calls and murder: rest often improves MG symptoms, so morning timing weakens the usual fatigue-based account. It does not rule MG out. ALS and stroke are acknowledged as substantial motor-pattern alternatives; prednisone does not make them impossible, but does not support treatment of either condition itself.
